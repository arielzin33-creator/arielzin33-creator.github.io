Hackathon AI-assisted build 2026

CirqHub

An AI-driven circular economy platform for physical market vendors, built with Base44 as an AI-assisted development platform.

01 Why I built it

Physical market vendors need a fast way to decide what happens to unsold surplus before it goes to waste. I co-designed a platform that tackles that in real time: the item lifecycle and REST API surface, food-safety check constraints, and two AI decision engines — one ranking surplus disposition (Donate / Barter / Reuse) and generating recipes, the other forecasting capacity to drive staffing and layout planning.

02 Tools & time

Built on Base44 as the AI-assisted development platform, with a PostgreSQL schema, an Express API, and an offline-first React front end — a custom offline-queue hook with local caching and background re-sync.

03 What challenged me

Directing AI-assisted implementation without accepting first-pass output — working iteratively on the generated PostgreSQL schema, API and offline sync logic rather than shipping whatever came out first. On the QA side: verifying disposition-state transitions, safety-constraint enforcement, and offline/online sync behaviour across both the vendor and operator surfaces, then code-reviewing the implementation for correctness and adherence to spec before sign-off.

04 What I learned

How to direct AI-assisted development against a clear specification — treating the generated code as a first draft to review and QA, not a finished product. Co-designing the two AI decision engines also meant translating a fuzzy real-world problem (what to do with surplus food) into constraints an engine could actually rank against.

Full-stack Solo

biz2code

A workflow system that guides software ideas through four human-approved phases, generating a Market Requirements Document, Product Requirements Document, and Business Plan.

01 Why I built it

Jumping straight from "idea" to "code" is exactly how AI-assisted tools produce confident, wrong output — there's no spec to hold the generation accountable to. biz2code forces the opposite order: an idea has to clear a Market Requirements Document, then a Product Requirements Document, then a Business Plan, with a human approving each phase before the next one starts. It's the spec-to-code discipline from my bootcamp work, turned into a tool instead of a habit.

02 Tools & time

TypeScript and React on the front end, Node.js/Express and PostgreSQL on the back end, with Python for parts of the document-generation pipeline. A solo, focused build outside bootcamp hours.

03 What challenged me

Designing the phase-gate logic itself — each stage needed explicit human approval before the next could generate, which meant the state machine had to be strict about what "approved" means and prevent a rejected phase from silently leaking into later documents.

04 What I learned

That structuring the spec is most of the work — once the MRD/PRD/Business Plan pipeline was solid, generating each document was the easy part. It's the same lesson as directing AI-assisted development generally: the quality of what comes out depends entirely on how clearly the input was defined.

Front-end Solo

Mythic Atlas

An interactive browser-based map overlaying real-world geography with mythological kingdoms, gods and creatures.

01 Why I built it

A palette-cleanser from the AI-assisted projects — something with no backend, no agent, just a map and an idea: what if the world's mythology lived at the real coordinates people place it at? Olympus over Greece, Asgard somewhere suitably northern. It's a portfolio piece that proves I can build a polished front-end experience from scratch, no framework doing the heavy lifting.

02 Tools & time

HTML5, CSS3 and vanilla JavaScript, with Leaflet.js driving the map and Leaflet.markercluster keeping dozens of kingdom/god/creature markers readable at every zoom level. Mythological locations and metadata are stored as GeoJSON.

03 What challenged me

Marker density. With enough kingdoms, gods and creatures placed on one map, overlapping pins turned into visual noise fast — which is exactly what Leaflet.markercluster is for, but tuning cluster radius and zoom thresholds so it clustered usefully instead of just hiding things took real iteration.

04 What I learned

How far vanilla JavaScript and a good mapping library go without React or a build step in the way. After a stretch of AI-directed, spec-driven work, writing every line of the interaction logic myself was a useful reset on the fundamentals.

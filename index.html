Hackathon 4-day delivery 2026

CirqHub

An AI-driven circular economy platform for physical market vendors, built with Base44 as an AI-assisted development platform.

01 The problem

Physical market vendors need a fast way to decide what happens to unsold surplus before it goes to waste — donate, barter or reuse — and how to plan staffing and layout for the day ahead. The constraint: four days, a team, and a demo at the end.

02 Scope & plan

Specification came before implementation: MRD, PRD, system architecture, database schema and REST API design. I then planned the four days day by day against a fixed deliverable list — eight-plus artifacts, from market research to the pitch deck — so the team always knew what "done today" meant.

03 My role

Team member owning planning and the spec: I co-designed the architecture, directed the AI-assisted implementation (Base44) against the spec, and ran QA and code review before sign-off.

04 Key decision & risk

An AI recommending what to do with food is a safety risk, so the controls went into the specification rather than being left to the build: deterministic calculations kept separate from LLM-generated text, a database-enforced allow-list of 12 external data sources, and hard-coded food-safety rules. QA then checked each against the spec before sign-off.

05 Outcome

Delivered on schedule: all eight-plus artifacts plus a working end-to-end demo, with vendor decisions, the operator capacity view and offline/online sync functioning together.

Solo Stage-gate workflow

biz2code

A workflow system that guides software ideas through four human-approved phases, generating a Market Requirements Document, Product Requirements Document, and Business Plan.

01 The problem

Teams jump from idea straight to build, and AI-assisted tools make that worse: without an agreed spec, there is nothing to hold the output accountable to — and no point at which anyone signs off.

02 Scope & plan

A stage-gate workflow for software ideas: each idea must pass a Market Requirements Document, then a Product Requirements Document, then a Business Plan, with a human approval gate between every phase. As with my other projects, MRD, PRD, architecture, schema and API design were written before implementation.

03 My role

Solo, end to end: product definition, planning, the gate rules and the delivery itself — the one project I also built entirely by hand.

04 Key decision & risk

Defining what "approved" means. A gate is only useful if a rejected phase can't silently leak into later documents, so the approval rules had to be explicit and enforced, not advisory — the same exit-criteria discipline I used moving materials from lab to pilot.

05 Outcome

Delivered as my bootcamp capstone: a working pipeline that takes a raw idea through four approved phases to a generated MRD, PRD and business plan. The lesson is a PM one: structuring the spec is most of the work.

Solo Fixed scope

Mythic Atlas

An interactive browser-based map overlaying real-world geography with mythological kingdoms, gods and creatures.

01 The problem

Mythology is tied to real places — Olympus over Greece, Asgard somewhere suitably northern — but there was no single, explorable map that placed it all at real coordinates.

02 Scope & plan

A deliberately tight boundary: a browser-only map with no backend and no framework, so the whole project could be finished rather than half-built. The content was planned up front as structured GeoJSON data — kingdoms, gods and creatures, each with its location and metadata.

03 My role

Solo — scope, content planning and delivery.

04 Key decision & risk

Information density. With enough entries on one map, markers turned into noise. Clustering fixes that, but set too aggressively it hides the content people came for — so the clustering thresholds were tuned iteratively against what a user actually needs to see at each zoom level.

05 Outcome

Delivered complete within the scope I set: a fully working interactive map, browsable end to end, with every planned layer finished and nothing left half-built.

Project lead Concept → pilot-ready 2026

HALIS

A physical AI reception platform — conversational AI plus automated hardware for visitor management, parcel lockers and access control in commercial and residential buildings — taken through the pre-MVP design stage.

01 The problem

How much of a building receptionist's job — visitor check-in, parcel and locker routing, access provisioning — can move to a hybrid of conversational AI and automated hardware, in commercial and residential buildings?

02 Scope & plan

Taking a concept to a pilot-ready definition before anything is built: a hybrid edge–cloud architecture broken into four layers (interaction, orchestration engine, cloud services, edge runtime), the core workflows, a Software Architecture Document, a PRD and a UI/UX design system — plus market sizing and competitive analysis to test whether it is worth building at all.

03 My role

Project lead: I owned the architecture and the documentation set from a blank page to pilot-readiness, and directed AI-assisted drafting of the architecture document, PRD and design system.

04 Key decision & risk

This system handles hardware and personal data, so mistakes are expensive to fix after the fact. I treated data protection as a scoping decision, not a later review: a DPIA/GDPR assessment and privacy-by-design constraints were part of the architecture work from day one, alongside the edge/cloud split.

05 Outcome

A concept documented to pilot-readiness: architecture, PRD, design system, business case and compliance assessment, ready for a pilot decision. The work is ongoing.

🔒 The repo is private and the project is still under ongoing work — not public yet.

Sole founder Product definition 2026

TrueTap

A Chrome extension that detects and neutralizes invisible click-intercepting overlays — transparent elements injected over real page content to hijack clicks into pop-unders, forced redirects, or unwanted downloads.

01 The problem

Streaming portals, file-hosting sites and ad-heavy blogs place invisible layers over real buttons and players to hijack clicks into pop-unders, forced redirects or unwanted downloads.

02 Scope & plan

Scoping was the core of this project. I defined the exact problem class — transparent click-capturing overlays, distinct from classic iframe clickjacking — and set hard constraints up front: a browser extension only, no backend, a minimal permission set, and nothing leaving the user's machine.

03 My role

Sole founder: problem framing, product mechanism, scope and constraints — then directing the AI-assisted build against that spec as the only person accountable for it.

04 Key decision & risk

Where to draw the line. Flagging too little leaves users exposed; flagging too much breaks legitimate sites. Deciding what counts as "suspicious enough to neutralize" was a product-risk decision that had to be made explicitly — not an implementation detail.

05 Outcome

A working extension delivered within the constraints set at the start: client-side only, minimal permissions, focused on one clearly defined problem.
